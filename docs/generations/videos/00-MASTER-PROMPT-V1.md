# MASTER PROMPT — VIDÉO 1  ·  Seedance 2.5  ·  le gabarit dont les 45 autres découlent

> **Ce qu'il fait de nouveau.** Les plans 1.2, 1.3 et 1.4 du script — jusqu'ici trois générations
> et deux raccords — deviennent **une seule prise de 30 s**. Les deux raccords disparaissent : la
> continuité n'est plus demandée, elle est mécanique.
>
> **Ce qu'il emprunte au prompt de référence.** La liste d'étapes datées, et surtout **la
> TRANSITION écrite entre chacune** : chaque étape sort sur un mouvement, la suivante entre sur ce
> même mouvement. Un vecteur se transmet mieux qu'une coordonnée. Les répliques sont **dans**
> l'étape où elles tombent, jamais dans une section à part qui peut dériver.
>
> **Ce qu'il garde de moi.** La géométrie canonique de la pièce, les exclusions par référence
> (dont l'audio), et la dernière frame chiffrée pour le plan suivant.

MATERIALS — @image1 IS THE FIRST FRAME OF THIS GENERATION. @sambefore is SAM. @maeva is MAEVE. @kitchen is THE KITCHEN. No video is attached: this is the head of the chain and it sets the light, the grain and the skin rendering every later shot will be matched to.
ROLES — From the first-frame image take the opening composition and the exact position, pose and prop state of everything in it; take nothing else from it, no border, no backdrop, no empty-room staging, no reference layout. From SAM, MAEVE and the kitchen reference take identity, face, build, wardrobe, architecture and materials exactly; take none of their pose, framing, lighting or staging.

STYLE — Kodak Vision3 500T 5219 tungsten colour negative, printed flat: cool naturalistic colour, shadows slightly blue-green and holding texture, skin understated and never rosy, highlights restrained and never blown, the faintest halation only at the window edge and nowhere else, moderate fine grain. Not glossy, not digital. EDITING GRAMMAR: THERE IS NO EDITING — one single unbroken take, 30 seconds, real time, no cut, no dissolve, no speed ramp, no slow motion. The only change of framing is the one the camera performs on its own feet in stage 4. Anamorphic 40 mm f/2.8 throughout: the frame tightens because the camera walks, never because it zooms.

AUDIO DESIGN — SFX only, and the sound thins as the shot goes. NO MUSIC ANYWHERE: no score, no drone, no ambient pad, at any point. First the room: the low sizzle and thin crackle of burnt bread, the breath of the gas dying, a small radio two metres away playing something too quiet to identify, one gull far off outside. Then the chair: the short dry scrape of its legs on tile as his weight leaves it, a small creak of wood. Then three steps in wool socks, almost silent, one soft drag on the third. Then the rustle of wool as the embrace closes, and the small unamplified sound of the kiss, barely there. Then a TRUE SILENCE holding the last five seconds, with only two sets of breathing that DO NOT fall into step, the pan ticking as it cools, and two low lines inside it. Clean dialogue, close, unprocessed.

CHARACTERS, and only what the reference sheets CANNOT carry — SAM: he narrates his own failures while they happen and laughs at himself; he touches people as he passes them; nothing guarded in the shoulders. HIS HANDS ARE CLEAN, NO INK ANYWHERE. MAEVE: dry, fast, affectionate, entirely unsentimental — her tenderness arrives as instruction, never as comfort. SHE WEARS NO SCARF OF ANY KIND: the red scarf of her reference sheet does not exist yet in the film. Face, build and wardrobe come from the references and are not restated here. NEITHER OF THEM PERFORMS AN EMOTION: the feeling is legible only through the physical events written below, and through nothing else. BUT THE ONE WHO IS LISTENING IS NEVER A DEAD FACE. Whenever one of them speaks, the other's face is ALIVE AND CHANGING THROUGHOUT THE LINE — never neutral, never held, never waiting for the line to end before it moves. THE REACTION LANDS ON THE WORD THAT CAUSES IT, INSIDE THE LINE, NEVER AFTER IT. It always starts in the eyes and reaches the mouth a beat later, never the reverse. And it is small: a millimetre of brow, a crease at the eye corner, a breath taken and not let out, the jaw easing, one blink that lands on a particular word. Micro-expression, not expression. No mugging, no reaction shot acting, no smile switched on at the end of a sentence.

THE ROOM, only what a single reference image cannot fix — THE GAS RANGE WITH THE SINGLE WINDOW DIRECTLY ABOVE IT is the far EAST wall, the sill just above the back burners, no cabinet over the range: it is the deepest landmark and the ONLY source of light. The sink, the cluttered counter and its small radio run along that same wall to its left; the open shelves of cream and blue crockery hold the north wall; THE DOORWAY TO THE DARK HALLWAY is in the south wall and stays empty for the whole take. The worn wooden table and its four mismatched chairs stand free in the middle of the room, roughly 3.6 m by 3.0 m. ON THE TABLE, exactly five things and never anything else: an open jar of orange marmalade with a teaspoon standing in it at the centre, a pat of butter on a cream-and-blue dish, one empty bowl at the near place, his steaming cup beside it, a second steaming cup at the far place with its chair EMPTY AND PUSHED IN. NO bread, NO toast, NO plate on the table — the only toast in existence is the two blackened slices in the cast-iron pan on the front-left burner, smoking very thinly under the window.

SCREEN DIRECTION, and it is not negotiable — the camera stays on the doorway side of the room for the whole take. SAM holds SCREEN-LEFT in every instant of this generation, MAEVE holds SCREEN-RIGHT, THE GAS RANGE AND ITS WINDOW are behind her, deepest in frame. This never inverts, not once.
LIGHT — ONE SOURCE ONLY, and the camera is NOT on its axis. The single window above the gas range is the only light in the room, but the camera sits about 35 degrees off it, on the doorway side: the window falls at the RIGHT FRAME EDGE, partly cropped, never centred behind them and never shot into. It is three-quarter back light, not contre-jour. CONSEQUENCE, AND IT IS THE POINT: BOTH FACES ARE FULLY MODELLED AND FULLY READABLE — a bright rim down one cheek and along the jaw, the other side of the face carried by real bounce, every pore, freckle, stray hair and eyelash sharp. NOBODY IS A SILHOUETTE AT ANY MOMENT and no face is lost to shadow. EXPOSURE IS SET ON THE SKIN, not on the window: the glass is the brightest thing in frame but it occupies less than a tenth of it and clips only in a small area, holding the sill and the frame bars as detail. THE FILL IS PHYSICAL, NOT ADDED: daylight bouncing off the pale scrubbed tabletop, the tiled floor and the cream crockery shelves opens the shadow side of both faces naturally — no lamp, no practical, no bounce card, no second source, nothing the room does not contain. COLOUR, and it follows the stock: this is tungsten-balanced negative shot in daylight, so the light is NEUTRAL TO FAINTLY COOL, a clean white morning with a little blue in the shadows. NOT gold, NOT amber, NOT orange, NOT golden hour, NOT sunset. The blue gas flame is the only saturated note and it dies in the first stage.

STAGE 1 (0:00-0:02) — Wide across the room from the west side, eye level 145 cm, operated and breathing, carried rather than nailed down. MAEVE stands at the range screen-right at x=68%, the window at the right frame edge behind her, her back three-quarter to camera, HER FACE FULLY LIT AND READABLE. SAM is SEATED at the table screen-left at x=30%, three-quarter to camera, turned toward her, a spoon in his hand. Nothing moves for a beat but the room: the thin smoke off the pan, the steam of the two cups turning in the bar of light. Then her right hand reaches down to the front-left knob and TURNS THE GAS OFF — the blue flame shrinks and dies, the sizzle sinking to a whisper. TRANSITION: the dying of the flame turns her; she pivots on the spot toward the room.
STAGE 2 (0:02-0:07) — Enters on that pivot: SHE COMES ROUND, her back settling against the range rail and both hands coming to rest on its edge either side of her hips, one ankle crossing loosely over the other, and she finds his eyes across the room. Half a confession and half a laugh, MAEVE: "I think I burned the toast." AND HIS FACE WORKS ALL THE WAY THROUGH HER LINE, not after it: on "think" his eyebrows lift a millimetre, on "burned" the creases at his eye corners deepen and one corner of his mouth has already gone before she reaches the last word, and he breathes out once through the nose. A beat of real silence, the pan crackling as it cools; HE HOLDS THE HALF-SMILE, it does not switch off. Then gentle, deadpan, no edge, SAM from the table without moving: "Why don't you use the toaster?" AND SHE IS ALREADY ANSWERING WITH HER FACE WHILE HE SPEAKS: her eyes narrow a fraction on "toaster", her head tips two degrees, she draws half a breath in on the last word and holds it. TRANSITION: her chin lifts a centimetre, the amusement reaching the eyes before the mouth.
STAGE 3 (0:07-0:13) — Enters on that lifted chin. Relaxed, matter-of-fact, the amusement rising on the last word, a real pause inside the line, MAEVE: "It's broken." — pause — "We're saving money." AND HIS FACE MOVES INSIDE HER LINE, NOT AFTER IT, AND THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT PIECE OF ACTING IN THE SHOT: on "broken" his eyebrows go up a millimetre and stay there, and he releases the breath he was holding; ACROSS THE PAUSE he does NOT go blank — his eyes drop half a centimetre to the pan and come back to her, and the corner of his mouth is already moving; on the word "money" the laugh reaches his eyes first and the mouth a beat later, and his head shakes once, two degrees, barely. HE IS NEVER EMOTIONALLY PAUSED, NOT FOR ONE FRAME, AND HE NEVER WAITS FOR HER TO FINISH BEFORE HIS FACE ANSWERS. A WARM SMILING SILENCE that runs nearly three full seconds: the two of them looking at each other across the kitchen, both wearing small growing smiles, her shoulders dropping half a centimetre, the steam of the two cups crossing the light between them. He sets the spoon down beside his bowl without a sound and FLATTENS BOTH PALMS ON THE TABLE EDGE, eyes never leaving her. TRANSITION: the weight goes into those two flat palms — the whole rise begins there, and it begins before he stands.
STAGE 4 (0:13-0:20) — Enters on the weight in his palms: HE RISES, AND THE WHOLE RISE IS SEEN. The fingers flatten and go a shade paler against the wood, the tendons standing on the backs of his hands, his shoulders coming forward over the table, his chin dropping, the chair legs rocking back a centimetre on the tile — and then he is up, and he stands a half-beat before he moves. HE CROSSES, three unhurried steps, his weight rolling heel to ball, the wool socks almost silent, one soft drag on the third step — AND THE CAMERA GOES WITH HIM, drifting right and forward at his pace, a beat behind him, an operator walking, never a dolly on rails, arriving by 0:20 at a tighter two-shot of the two of them chest-up, BOTH FACES MODELLED AND SHARP, the window cropped at the right frame edge behind them, where it settles and breathes for the rest of the take. TRANSITION: he stops face to face with her and the camera stops with him, both of them still moving very slightly inside the frame.
STAGE 5 (0:20-0:24) — Enters on that arrival, close. A HELD BEAT, FACE TO FACE, and it is the whole point of the shot: her chin tips up two centimetres to keep his eyes, his head tilts one degree, she breathes in through the nose and it lifts her shoulders a centimetre and lets them down. Neither of them speaks. Then HIS ARMS GO AROUND HER AND THE MOVEMENT IS SEEN WHOLE — the right arm first and low, sliding around the small of her back, the left following a beat later and higher, his hands coming flat with the fingers spread and the wool of her sweater gathering under his palms. Her two hands come flat on his chest, one slightly higher than the other. AND HE KISSES HER: unhurried, mouths closed, his head tipping a few degrees to clear her nose, her chin lifting to meet it, both pairs of eyes closing — a kiss between two people who have kissed ten thousand times, short, certain, entirely unperformed. TRANSITION: the kiss ends by itself and they come apart barely two centimetres, still holding each other, eyes still closed.
STAGE 6 (0:24-0:30) — Enters on that small parting, their faces still almost touching, eyes still closed, breathing on each other. Whispered, without opening her eyes and without moving her head, her lips the only thing in her face that moves, MAEVE: "Love you." AND HE ANSWERS WITH HIS FACE WHILE SHE SAYS IT, EYES STILL CLOSED: his eyebrows draw in a millimetre on the word, the jaw eases, the mouth softens. A breath; HIS CHEST LIFTS ONCE UNDER HER TWO HANDS and her hands ride it up and down. Then LOWER than she was, certain, just as quietly, SAM: "Love you more." AND HER FACE TAKES IT AS HE SPEAKS, eyes still closed: the smallest tightening at the eye corners, the mouth curving a millimetre on "more". AND THEN HE RESTS HIS HEAD AGAINST HERS — his forehead coming down onto hers and settling there, the weight of it real, her head taking it and giving a centimetre. Held to the end: forehead against forehead, both pairs of eyes closed, NOBODY MOVES except their breathing, and THE TWO BREATHS DO NOT FALL INTO STEP — his slower and deeper, hers shallower and quicker, crossing each other. One last thin thread of smoke rising from the cooling pan.
THE SPOKEN LINES OF THIS GENERATION ARE EXACTLY THESE FOUR AND NO OTHERS: "I think I burned the toast." / "Why don't you use the toaster?" / "It's broken." "We're saving money." / "Love you." / "Love you more." — no line is repeated, no line is added, nobody else speaks, and there is no voice-over. Between 0:13 and 0:25 NOBODY SPEAKS AT ALL: no word, no murmur, no breath shaped like speech.

ENDING FRAME, the exact state this generation hands over — The two of them chest-up, both faces fully modelled and readable in the clean morning light, forehead resting against forehead, both pairs of eyes closed; her two hands flat on his chest one slightly higher than the other; his arms wrapped around her waist with the wool of her sweater gathered under his palms; SAM centre-left at x=44% filling 72% of frame height, MAEVE centre-right at x=58% filling 70%; one last thin thread of smoke rising from the cooling pan beside them under the window; the laid table untouched behind them at the lower right frame edge.
AVOID — a blown white window filling the frame, shooting straight into the window, contre-jour, either face in silhouette, either face lost to shadow, a dark unreadable face, heavy halation, bloom, glow, lens flare, sun streaks, god rays, visible light beams, dust-in-a-sunbeam, golden hour, orange or amber cast, a warm filter, over-saturation, HDR, waxy or plastic skin, smoothed skin, an open-mouthed kiss, a deep or passionate kiss, a long kiss, the kiss or the embrace played theatrical, a dead or frozen face on whoever is listening, a face that only reacts after the line ends, a smile switched on at the end of a sentence, mugging, reaction-shot acting, tears, crying, sadness, any spoken line other than the five written above, a toaster, any toaster visible anywhere, a flame under the pan after stage 1, the toast leaving the pan, toast or bread or a plate on the table, food being served, a knife or utensil in anyone's hands, a third person in the kitchen, a silhouette in the doorway, a red scarf, a scarf of any kind on the woman, any red anywhere, a cut of any kind, a dissolve, a zoom, a crash zoom, a whip pan, slow motion, a speed ramp, the camera pushing in on its own, music of any kind, on-screen text, subtitles, captions, either of them looking at camera, visible camera rigs.
